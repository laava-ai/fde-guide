# 1. Observe and scope

The service desk lead says, “Can AI route our tickets?” A dispatcher reads each new request and picks a queue. A poor handoff adds a second wait for the person who raised it. Before choosing a model, find out how the dispatcher handles a difficult ticket.

## Watch one decision

Ask the dispatcher to walk through a recent request from arrival to acceptance by the next team. Ask what they saw, which detail affected their choice and how they found out whether the queue accepted it. Then ask for an exception. A dashboard can show a transfer. The dispatcher can explain why it happened.

In this fictional exercise, the billing team handles invoices and charges. The access team handles sign-in and password problems. Requests that mention both, lack enough detail, concern another topic or report suspicious activity go to a human dispatcher. A reviewer can correct every proposed route.

## Write the trial brief

| Question | Example answer |
|---|---|
| Who uses the result? | The dispatcher who assigns the first queue. |
| What changes on screen? | Show a suggested queue beside the request. The dispatcher confirms or changes it. |
| What stays under human control? | Review all uncertain and sensitive requests. Only the dispatcher can move a ticket. |
| What would justify a pilot? | Correct suggestions on a small labelled set, followed by evidence that dispatchers spend less time per request. |
| Who can stop the trial? | The desk lead can remove suggestions and restore manual routing. |

Read requests T01 and T05 in the [fictional case file](../exercises/intake/cases.csv). Both mention an invoice. Explain why the second needs human review. Write one observation that would change the scope of the trial, and name the person who could confirm it.
